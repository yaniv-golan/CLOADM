WHY THIS EXISTS

A Bar Mitzvah present, 1983.

I was thirteen, in Israel, when my parents gave me a Dragon 64. Not the 32: the 64. It was like magic. This emulator is my way of switching it on again, exactly as it was, and handing it to anyone who remembers that feeling.

Yaniv Golan, maintainer
  1. 1981 · ONE LINE
    10 PRINT "HELLO"█

    Dad's Casio FX-702P

    A programmable calculator with one line of screen and a BASIC interpreter. I learned to code from its manual. You wrote a line and it ran it: that was the first magic trick.

  2. THEN · THE SCHOOL BASEMENT

    A ZX81, after school

    An afternoon programming course in the basement of our school. A real screen, and a keyboard you pressed more than typed on. Enough to know I wanted more.

  3. 1983 · AGE 13
    64K BASIC INTERPRETER 1.0
    OK

    The Dragon 64

    Colour, sound, 64K and a proper keyboard. Countless hours, and PEEKs and POKEs I still know by heart.

  4. CARRIED HOME

    Magazines from America

    No internet, and little printed in Israel. Anyone travelling to the US came back with my magazines and books in their suitcase.

    Read them again: The Rainbow → Dragon User, all 69 issues →
POKE 65495,0 :REM DOUBLE SPEED, IF YOURS TOOK IT POKE 65494,0 :REM BACK TO NORMAL EXEC :REM DRAGON 64 INTO 64K MODE ?PEEK(25)*256+PEEK(26) :REM PROGRAM START

Remember yours?

The emulator has a POKE book: the addresses people memorised, each one live, including the scary ones. Nothing here can break. Type it and the panel shows which chip register it hit.

Tapes that sound like tapes

.CAS files load through the real 1200/2400 Hz signal, with the motor relay; fast loading skips the wait. WAV recordings from the attic load too, and what you CSAVE comes back as .CAS or WAV. Switch to real speed to hear it load at the original pace.

Four drives, real disks

DragonDOS, Delta, RS-DOS, with your own disk ROM. VDK, DSK and DMK images, kept in your library: what the machine writes goes back into the stored image, and Download gives you the file.

OLD HARDWARE, NEW TRICKS

Same Dragon. Forty years apart.

In 1983, a computer that talked back was a magic trick: a few hundred lines of keyword matching, ELIZA-style. LIZA is that trick, rebuilt for this project. Now the same 6809 holds a real conversation: firmware written for this project gives the Dragon a lowercase font it never had and carries your words to Claude.

ROMS · BRING YOUR OWN

The machine is ours. The BASIC is theirs.

The ROMs that make a Dragon say OK are copyrighted by Microsoft, Tandy, Dragon Data and others. CLOADM doesn't ship them, download them or fetch them for you. You add your own ROMs once, and they stay in this browser.

  1. 1
    Dump your own. If the machine is still in the attic, its ROMs are the cleanest source.
  2. 2
    Or find the files. The community-run Color Computer Archive keeps the sets XRoar uses (where to get ROMs). It isn't part of this project and we never connect to it; whether you may download from it depends on where you live and on the rights holders' terms.
  3. 3
    Drop the lot on the emulator. Files, a folder or a ZIP, any names. CLOADM recognises each ROM by its contents and sets up every machine it fits. CLOADM never uploads your ROMs and never puts them in share links or exported settings. If you use Claude, what it reads from the machine's memory (ROM areas included) is sent to Kapai (or to Anthropic, with your own key) with your conversation.
