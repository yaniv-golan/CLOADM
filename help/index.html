Commands and shortcuts

Press Ctrl K (⌘ K on a Mac) anywhere, or choose Commands… in the app bar's links (behind Menu on a phone), to open the command box. With nothing typed it lists every command; type to filter them, or type a question for Claude. Commands run in the page and are never sent to Claude.

Typing finds a command by the start of a word of its name, then by other words it answers to ("delete rom" finds Your ROMs), and forgives one wrong, missing or extra letter in a word of four letters or more ("rset" finds Reset). The matched part is highlighted; ↓ on the last row goes back to the first, and ↑ before choosing goes to the last. Every command is always listed: one that doesn't apply now (Eject the tape with no tape in, Switch to the model you are using) says why on its second line, and choosing it tells you why instead of running.

Shortcuts

  • Ctrl K (⌘ K on a Mac): the command box
  • Ctrl B: switches between the Claude chat and your BASIC session
  • Esc = BREAK · Home = CLEAR · Backspace = ← (on a Mac without Home: Fn+← or Fn+Delete, as under Keys)
  • Esc in the full-screen chat: while a reply streams it stops the reply; with a line typed it drops the line; on an empty, idle prompt it leaves the chat
  • Esc while Claude is paused: stops Claude

Claude: getting an API key

Where? Get a key at console.anthropic.com, under API keys, and paste it into the key field beside the chat.
What does it cost? Use is billed to your Anthropic account: see pricing.
No key? None is needed: Kapai's included AI answers, and the credits show beside the chat. Or use CLOADM inside Claude: see Use with Claude.

What Claude can do

Reads (always): the machine's model and settings, the screen's text (not graphics), memory, and disassembly.
Acts (only with your permission): types, presses BREAK, POKEs memory, loads BASIC programs, assembles and loads machine code, and runs programs. A restore point is made first.
What Claude reads goes to Kapai (or to Anthropic, with your own key). That includes the machine's memory, so any ROM areas it reads are sent too. CLOADM itself never uploads your ROMs.

Which machine is mine?

Every machine CLOADM emulates, as the machine menu groups them: what each one is, and the ROMs it takes, by the names Your ROMs uses. CLOADM never includes ROMs: each machine needs its own, dumped from your own.

ROMs

Do I need them? Only for BASIC and programs that use it. The Welcome menu, LIZA and the colour demo run without.
Where do I get them? Dump them from your own machine, or see where to get ROMs. They're copyrighted by Microsoft, Tandy, Dragon Data and others; check your rights. CLOADM never downloads them.
Which file goes where? It doesn't matter. Drop them all; CLOADM recognises each by its contents.
Can I share a snapshot that uses my ROMs? Yes. A share link never holds a ROM or a cartridge: it names the ones the snapshot used, and the person opening it needs their own copies of the same versions. Without them, CLOADM says which ROMs to add and opens the snapshot once they're in.
Do my ROMs ever leave my browser? CLOADM never uploads them and never puts them in share links or exported settings. If you use Claude, what it reads from the machine's memory (ROM areas included) is sent to Kapai (or to Anthropic, with your own key) as part of your conversation.

WYRMFIRE

What is it? A 3D game for the Dragon 64 that plays a Doom-format level from a disk: WYRMFIRE 0.1.0, free software under the GNU GPL version 3 or later (the licence, where the source is, github.com/yaniv-golan/wyrmfire). It runs without ROMs: open Programs and choose WYRMFIRE, or link to play/?start=wyrmfire.
Keys. Up and Down move, Left and Right turn, Z and X strafe, Space fires or punches, E opens a door or uses the exit, 1 2 3 pick the fist, pistol or shotgun, D toggles the wall hatching, C on the title shows the credits, Enter skips a tune and restarts after the end. On a touch screen the Pad has FIRE and USE as its two big buttons.
The level. Map E1M1 of Freedoom 0.13.0, Copyright © 2001-2024 Contributors to the Freedoom project, under the BSD 3-Clause licence (Level: Freedoom (BSD)): the licence, credits, music credits. The level disk carries the credit and the licence too: C on the title screen shows the credits (Enter goes on).
Your own DOOM? Have DOOM (the 1993 game)? Drop your DOOM1.WAD or DOOM.WAD on the page, or use Your ROMs → Your game files. Your WAD stays in this browser: it's converted here and never uploaded. Only the converted level is kept, never the WAD, and it is never in a share link, the Library or a settings export. DOOM is a registered trademark of ZeniMax Media Inc. CLOADM and WYRMFIRE are independent projects, not affiliated with or endorsed by id Software, ZeniMax Media or Microsoft. References to DOOM describe file compatibility only.
What it does and doesn't do. It plays one level: map E1M1, the first level of episode 1 (of Freedoom, or of your own DOOM1.WAD or DOOM.WAD); not the other maps, and not Doom II-format WADs. It runs on a Dragon 64 (64K, PAL) without ROMs, reading its level from an 80-track double-sided disk through a WD2797 DragonDOS-compatible controller, which it drives itself: the level stays in the drive and parts of it load as you move. Walls and floors are line drawings; the monsters, the weapons (fist, pistol, shotgun) and two status-bar faces are 1-bit sprites, its sounds 6-bit samples at 4 kHz, its music the opening seconds of three tunes, one voice each. Doors and the exit work. Speed: 6.4 pictures a second while you stay in one part of the level, with a pause of about 1 second (49 fields median, 63 at most) each time a new part loads from the disk: measured in CLOADM headless (its Node API, a Dragon 64 at 50 fields a second) with WYRMFIRE 0.1.0's route bot walking E1M1 of the Freedoom level from the start to the exit (342 pictures in 3,692 fields, 4.6 a second overall).
The Freedoom disk, to keep. The level disk CLOADM plays, as VDK or DSK (JVC) (720K, 80 tracks, 2 sides), with the engine WYRMFIRE.BIN, its GPL and Freedoom's licence. Level: Freedoom (BSD). A level made from your own WAD is never offered for download.
On a real Dragon 64? Untested on real hardware. It needs a WD2797 DragonDOS-compatible disk controller (the Dragon Data cartridge or a compatible one) and an 80-track double-sided drive: a Gotek drive emulator given the .dsk, or a real 720K floppy written from it. Load WYRMFIRE.BIN as a DragonDOS binary (it loads and starts at $0C00) with the level disk in drive 0, and keep the disk in. DriveWire can't serve it: the engine reads the disk through the controller's registers itself.
What's off while it runs? Snapshots. With your own WAD, also clips, screenshots and Claude reading the screen or memory: that level is id Software's game data.

Sharing a moment

Short link or long link? The long link ("Long link (works offline; nothing is uploaded)") carries the whole moment after the # in the address, so it is a few thousand characters long, and nothing is uploaded. The short link is about 50: the moment is sealed in your browser and kept on s.cloadm.dev, and the key that opens it is the part after the #, which never reaches the server. Short links need the internet: offline, or where they can't be made, Copy link gives the long link instead, and "More ways to share" always has it.
Who can open a short link? Links don't expire. A link reported for abuse can be removed. Only people with the link can open it; the server can't read it. The preview picture, when "Show a preview picture" is on, is public: anyone with the address can see it, even without the part after the #.
Can a moment on my ROMs have a short link? No: short links are only for machines running without ROMs or cartridges. The long link works; whoever opens it adds their own ROMs.
How do I delete a short link? "Delete this link" on the Share screen, right after making it (this browser keeps what deleting needs). Copies already cached on the way may still open for up to a day.

Why? Installed, CLOADM opens in its own window, away from the browser's tabs and address bar, and works offline. The files it keeps (ROMs, tapes, disks, programs) are the same ones either way.

Quick fixes

Black screen, no OK. A required ROM is missing or not recognised. A box on the screen says which; Add ROMs shows them.
No sound. Click the screen or press a key once; browsers keep sound off until you do.
Tape won't load. Check it's for this machine, and undo any speed POKE first: POKE 65494,0 undoes POKE 65495,0, and POKE 65496,0 undoes POKE 65497,0.
Disk shows nothing. DIR and the other disk commands need the machine's disk ROM: the DragonDOS or Delta cartridge on a Dragon, Disk BASIC on a CoCo. Without it a disk still goes in, for programs that read it directly, and the drives under the screen say so. Check the disk is for this machine too: DragonDOS disks are for a Dragon, RS-DOS disks for a CoCo, and CLOADM says which a disk is.
Wrong symbols. Keys type what they say, so shifted symbols follow your keyboard, not the original layout.
ROMs gone in a new browser. Use Save ROM pack in the old one and drop the file on CLOADM in the new one.

In the emulator it is also in the command box (Ctrl K).
